Exchange doctrine

XOSCAL Exchange is a verification substrate, not a share button.

  • Publishing does not imply trusted.
  • Receiving does not imply verified.
  • Verification is the receiver's, re-run locally.
  • Reconciliation is by hash, not custody transfer.
  • Disclosure requires explicit class.
  • Inbound claims enter as imported-unverified until VerifyClaim passes locally.

Evidence Cartography — the card system

Every external artifact becomes a card only when it can trace to an OSCAL object, claim, assessment result, back-matter resource, mapping edge, or declared extension manifest. No card may be decorative.

Canonical card families

Control · Claim · Evidence · Finding · Gap · Conflict · POA&M · Mapping Edge · Source · Export Bundle. Each card answers: what is this, what does it claim, what does it support, what is its proof state, what can safely happen next.

Interoperability dialect cards

External assurance ecosystems linked into OSCAL through back-matter, assessment evidence, mapping edges, or declared extension manifests. When OSCAL can carry it natively, use OSCAL. When it cannot, declare lossiness and link via extension manifest.

Defensive experience patterns

Gap is honest. Stale evidence is visible. Contradiction uses alarm. Quarantine holds. Redaction is a visible transformation. Lossiness must be declared. Import never means trust.

Extension manifest

When OSCAL cannot natively represent external semantics, the extension manifest makes the gap explicit — with schema, reason, lossiness, and OSCAL linkage.
